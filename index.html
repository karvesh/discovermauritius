A Prayer in Four Colours

In quiet layers, the island breathes a hymn woven in red, blue, yellow and green. Each band not merely seen, but felt.

Red rises first, a memory carried in the soil, of hands that toiled, of voices that refused silence. It is the pulse of ancestors, the echo of sacrifice, a reminder that freedom is never given, only earned.

Blue follows like a whispering infinity, the embrace of the Indian Ocean, endless and watchful. It feeds, it carries, it protects. In its depths lie both journey and belonging, the island cradled in its quiet strength.

Yellow breaks through, a dawn that never tires, light spilling over the land like a promise. It is hope made visible, the sun that warms tomorrow, the golden breath of a future still unfolding.

Green settles the soul, alive in every field, every leaf, every breeze. It is the earth’s gentle offering, fertile and faithful, a living testament to renewal, to life that insists on growing.

And beyond colour, there is unity a deeper rhythm beneath the hues, where many paths, many faiths, many stories find their place in one shared sky.

So the flag 🇲🇺 does not merely fly it remembers, it protects, it illuminates, it nurtures. A quiet prayer in motion, carried by the wind of an island that knows both its past and its becoming.

A Strategic Location

Mauritius, in the Indian Ocean with Port Louis as its capital, bridges Africa and Asia. First encountered by the Dutch East India Company, it was later colonised by France, which brought enslaved Africans. Under United Kingdom rule, slavery was abolished and Indian indentured labourers were introduced, shaping its diverse culture.

71% Sangat Population

The central plateau, also known as Plaine Wilhems is home to the majority of the Sangat community, with the SOS centre located in Quatre Bornes.

GDP: $15.73 Billion in 2025

From the bustling port of Port Louis to the innovation-driven Cybercity of Ebène, Mauritius thrives as a dynamic hub of trade, finance, and digital transformation.

1.27 Million Souls

Our "Rainbow Nation" is a vibrant multicultural nation, where communities of Indian, African, Chinese, and European heritage coexist, creating a rich blend of cultures, languages, and traditions.

A Sea of Sugarcane

Though Mauritius spans only 2,040 km², its fields tell a bigger story: sugarcane continues to cover almost 40% of the island, weaving agriculture into its identity.

Along the Turquoise Shore

From the iconic Le Morne to the untamed south, our coastline unfolds as a playground of coral reefs, while the island’s southern trails lead adventurers through dramatic landscapes and unforgettable hikes.

A Volcanic Legacy

Rising over 800 meters, these peaks are the eroded remnants of a shield volcano formed nearly ten million years ago. They represent the oldest geological phase of Mauritius, shaped by millions of years of tropical weathering.

The Ascent

Le Pouce

Climb to the top of Le Pouce, one of the most famous mountains in Mauritius. Standing 812m high, this trail was once hiked by Charles Darwin. The walk takes you through a beautiful forest and is easy to follow. At the very end, there is a short, steep climb to the peak where you may need to use your hands for balance.

HIKER EXPERIENCE

Moderate Hike • 2-3 Hours

The Legend

Pieter Both

Famous for the huge, round rock at the top that looks like a human head. For hikers, this is a technical and exciting climb. To reach the very top and touch the big balancing rock, you must use climbing ropes and a professional guide.

HIKER EXPERIENCE

Advanced Hike • Ropes Required

The Tale of Santaka

Local lore speaks of a milkman named Santaka who discovered fairies dancing upon the summit. They granted him a wish on one condition: his silence. Unable to contain the magic, he shared the secret with his village. Today, his head and body remain perched atop the mountain—an eternal reminder of a broken promise.
